Source: `Documents/ABS 2E Layouts 7.pdf`, 224 pages. Every page number below is the
**printed** page number as shown on the page, which in this PDF is always the PDF page
index plus one.

Quotations marked with `>` and a page number are **verbatim**, character for character,
including the book's own typos and punctuation. Anything not in a blockquote is my own
summary unless it is explicitly labelled as a quote.

## 2. The solo system, end to end

### 2.1 What Junkspace is

> As Junkspace is a solo game mode, we have to tweak a few of the Core Rules, and introduce
> some completely new ones, to manage the hidden and automated enemies. You lead a small
> flotilla of ships, called your 'Outfit'. You are in debt to a dangerous local gangster and
> must complete Jobs while being menaced by various Aggressors. (p.195)

> In Junkspace, we zoom the action into a single system, around a star called Jura, to follow
> scavengers, raiders, and free-traders as they scratch out an existence among the
> post-apocalyptic debris. The Junkspace Era is a solo game mode of hunting for scraps in the
> detritus of an isolated system. It can be played as a one-off solo game but is designed to
> be experienced best as a solo narrative campaign, following the fate of your rag-tag outfit
> as it tries to survive among the ruins of Jura. (p.194)

The catch-all tiebreaker for the whole mode:

> In Junkspace, if you can't figure out precisely what the rules are telling you to do:
> choose the interpretation or outcome that disadvantages you the most. (p.195)

### 2.2 Building the Outfit (once, at campaign start)

> At the start of a Junkspace campaign, you take out a loan of ¢30k to create an Outfit, and
> then fly Jobs to clear your debt. (p.201)

> To start your career in Junkspace, you create your Outfit. You get ¢30k with which to buy
> ships, and you can buy up to a maximum of 5 ships (as any more gets prohibitively expensive
> in berthing fees). (p.201)

> These are the stock ship classes you select from at the start of the campaign. Their costs
> are listed here in thousands of Juran credits (¢k), rather than the billions of Galactic
> Universal Credits listed elsewhere in this rulebook (¢bn). (p.202)

> For each ship, select a Pilot Class: Gunner, Hauler or Junker. (p.202)

> At the start of the campaign, all your pilots have an Initiative Value of 2D6. (p.202)

> In Junkspace, all units consist of exactly one ship. (p.195)

Ship classes, pilot classes and their starting perks are tabulated in section 3 below.

### 2.3 Per-game setup

> To set up a game of Junkspace:
> 1 Generate Jobs
> 2 Set Up Play Area
> 3 Set Up Blip Markers
> 4 Set Up Objectives
> 5 The Setup Glitches (p.203)

Note that the printed ordered list puts Blip Markers third and Objectives fourth, while the
body sections below it on pp.203-204 run Play Area, then **Set Up Objectives**, then **Set Up
Blip Markers**, then The Set Up Glitches. That is my observation, not the book's.

**Generate Jobs**

> Each game, there are three Jobs available to complete. Before the game, get a deck of
> standard playing cards and shuffle all the cards of one suit together. Flip three cards to
> generate the Jobs for this game. (p.203)

**Play area**

> The game is played on a 3' by 3' board. This fixed size is necessary to be able to create
> and control the solo gameplay. Place an Entry Jump Point anywhere with 3" of one corner;
> place the Exit Jump Point anywhere with 3" of the opposite corner. Divide the table
> diagonally in half, so that the Exit point is at the back of the Hostile half of the table.
> (p.203)

("anywhere with 3"" is the book's own wording; it means "within 3"". That is my reading.)

> Each game has an Entry Jump Point and an Exit Jump Point. You can only Jump In via the
> Entry Jump Point. You can Jump Out via either Jump Point. (p.196)

**Objectives**

> Set up any additional Objectives, as specified by the Jobs. (p.204)

> Several of the rules below refer to 'Objectives'. An Objective within these rules is one of
> the *things* used by a Job. It could be an object, such as a Facility or ComAat, or a
> Job-related asset token, such as a Kraken Heart. Carried tokens and Scrap tokens don't count
> as Objectives for this, they are invisible to the game's AI. (p.196)

("ComAat" is printed exactly like that in the book. I checked the rendered page, it is not an
extraction artefact.)

**Blips**

> You will need to manufacture some Blip markers. These are eight 30mm flat markers or
> counters, numbered from 1 to 8 on one side, and indistinguishable from each other when
> turned over. (p.197)

> During setup, you shuffle the Blip markers together, deploy them facedown (without looking
> at the numbers on them) anywhere in the Hostile table half, with each at least 7" from any
> other Blip marker and 2" from all table edges. (p.197)

> Shuffle the Blip markers together, and deploy them facedown (without looking at the numbers)
> in the Hostile table half, according to this diagram. Each should be 7" from all other Blip
> markers. (p.204)

The p.204 diagram shows eight dots inside the shaded Hostile half, strung along and just
inside the dividing diagonal, joined by short white lines, with a key reading "Blip •" and
"7" ——". My reading is that the diagram illustrates the 7" spacing rather than mandating
fixed positions, but the book does not say so.

**The Setup Glitches**

> After you have set up the Objectives and the Blip markers, scatter everything D10". Roll a
> D10 for each Objective and Blip marker and push them the rolled number of inches in the
> direction indicated by the pointy top of the D10. Stop if it comes within 2" of a table
> edge. (Jump Points don't get scattered.) (p.204)

> Glitching your set up stops you from overly optimising the initial setup in your favour, and
> adds some pleasing randomness for you to respond to. (p.204, NOTE box)

### 2.4 The opposing force

The opposition is not a fleet you build. It is eight face-down Blip markers whose contents are
unknown until revealed, plus whatever a Job spawns.

> In Junkspace, you try to complete Jobs, but everywhere there are hostile forces arrayed
> against you. The pirate ships that menace you are controlled by AI behaviour rules. (p.195)

> Hostile facilities don't attack Hostile ships. (p.195)

> The Jura system is filled with violent and desperate people, as well as some crooked and
> criminal ones too. Wherever you go to try and make a spacebuck, you can be certain that your
> jetstreams will be dogged by pirates of one stripe or another. The "Blip" numbers in the
> tables indicate which Blip marker reveals a single ship of this class. (p.211)

> You should feel free to invent your own sets of enemy ships to harass your solo games. Maybe
> take some of the ship classes from the main factions and borrow a special rule from an HVP.
> (p.211, NOTE box)

So the force composition is fixed and known in aggregate (four Pirate Starfighters, two Pirate
Gunships, one Pirate Frigate, one Pirate Cruiser) but its distribution across the table is
hidden. That inference is mine; the book only gives the Blip-number-to-class mapping.

**Revealing**

> Blip tokens within 6" of your ships are revealed. To reveal a Blip, flip it over without
> altering its position, and look up its number against your Aggressor list. Replace the Blip
> marker with the indicated ship, facing the nearest of your ships. If this happens during the
> Tactical Phase, the revealed ship Ambushes you. (p.198)

> If a Hostile unit is revealed during the End Phase, it doesn't Ambush you (and doesn't take
> any actions until the new round). (p.198)

> When a Hostile unit is revealed in the Tactical Phase, it Ambushes you: your activation
> ends. Play passes to the Hostiles, meaning they might get to immediately activate the
> revealed ship and attack. (p.198)

**Blips moving**

> When you use the Open Fire action to attack something, Glitch all Blip tokens within 6" of
> that target (after resolving your Open Fire action). (p.198)

> When instructed to 'Glitch' a Blip token, roll a D6 on this table and carry out the
> instructions: (p.199)

The Glitch table and its bullet definitions are reproduced in section 3.

### 2.5 How the Hostiles behave

> Hostile ships follow set behaviour when activated. They are mostly aggressive but may
> attempt to defend Objectives. (p.199)

> When you activate a Hostile ship, roll a D6 on this table and carry out the instructions:
> (p.199)

> All of the words in this table refer to 'routines' that are described below. (p.199)

The Random Behaviour table and the full Routines text are reproduced verbatim in section 3.

> When it is the Hostiles' turn to activate, you activate the unactivated Hostile ship with the
> largest Silhouette. If there are multiple ships that match these criteria, pick the one
> closest to your ships. If still tied, randomise between them. During their activations, you
> control the Hostiles, but you follow their Behaviour rules (see page XX). (p.200, repeated
> word for word on p.205)

> Hostiles always make passive attacks if they can. If they have multiple passive targets, they
> attack the Strongest Enemy. (p.200)

> Note: Hostile Ships suffer from Inertial Strain and Easy Target as normal. (p.200, NOTE box)

> Take No Prisoners: Pirate ships re-roll the first miss when attacking with auxiliary
> weapons. (p.211)

### 2.6 The round

> Because Junkspace is played solo, there are some changes to the way the Phases work. Unless
> noted below, assume the game works exactly as in the Core Rules. (p.205)

**Command Phase**

> Command Tokens: You gain 5 CMD tokens. As your outfit gains experience, this number can
> increase. (p.205)

> You do not make an Initiative Check: the Hostiles activate the first ship. (p.205)

**Jump Phase**

> Only you act in the Jump Phase: the Hostiles arrive via Blip markers. (p.205)

**Tactical Phase**

> The Tactical Phase works as normal, alternating the activation of battlegroups, starting
> with the Hostiles. You are free to form battlegroups as normal, but the Hostiles don't form
> battlegroups, activating one ship at a time. (p.205)

> During games of Junkspace, you have access to a new command:
> Seize Initiative (1 CMD): Junkspace only. At the start of the Tactical Phase, spend 1 CMD
> token to activate the first battlegroup, before the Hostiles get a chance to activate a
> unit. (p.205)

**End Phase**

> The End Phase has some new rules in Junkspace:
> Increase the Alert Level by 1 (it starts the game at 1).
> Glitch the Blips (rolling on the D6 table for each). (p.206)

### 2.7 The game clock and how a game ends

> At the start of the game, your Alert Level is 1. The game ends when the Alert Level hits 10.
> Track it with a D10. Things that can affect the Alert Level:
> In each End Phase, increase the Alert Level by 1.
> When you reveal a Hostile Mass 2-3 ship, increase the Alert Level by 1.
> When you destroy a Hostile Mass 2-3 ship, decrease the Alert Level by 2. (p.195)

> As you progress through the campaign, the starting Alert Level increases, making the game
> more difficult. If you have less than ¢25k Debt remaining, the Alert Level starts the game at
> 2. If you have cleared your Debt completely, the Alert Level starts at 3. (p.195)

> The game ends at the end of any Round if either of the following are true:
> The Alert Level is 10 (or higher).
> You have no ships in play. (p.206)

> Don't feel like you have to fully complete all the jobs if it's getting too dangerous: get
> out with what you can! (p.206, NOTE box)

Jobs also modify the Alert Level: Job 5 (+1 on first damage to the Derelict Facility), Job 7
(+2 on the scan), Job 8 (+1 on the first successful scan), Job Q (+1 per ComSat first
scanned). Job 3 pays out on a sliding scale keyed to the current Alert Level.

### 2.8 Scoring and winning

There are no victory points in Junkspace. You score credits in ¢k, and the only score that
matters is your Debt.

> You start the campaign with a Debt of ¢30k. After each game, you reduce your Debt by your
> earnings, with the aim of clearing your Debt and enjoying a life without a crippling
> financial straightjacket. If you clear your debt within 8 games, you have won the campaign.
> If, after 8 games, you still have outstanding debt, some very unpleasant people pay a visit
> to your space dock and you lose the campaign (and your ships). (p.201)

> If you would like a more difficult solo campaign: try to clear your debt in 6 games or
> increase your debt to ¢45k. (p.201, NOTE box)

> You earn credits by completing Jobs during the game. Reduce your Debt by the amount you
> earned in this game. When you have cleared your Debt, you win the campaign. (p.211)

### 2.9 Post-game

> After each game, you begrudgingly hand over the money you've earned from your Jobs to pay
> down your Debts, and spend the little that's left on fuel, berthing, coin-operated
> laundromats, replicated ramen and local moonshine. Pilots that survived the game gain
> experience, in the form of Perks. (p.211)

> Destroyed ships are not lost - they are assumed to be recovered and repaired before the next
> game. However, if a ship survives the game, its pilot can gain a Perk. The Perks available to
> a pilot depend on their class. A pilot cannot have the same Perk twice. (p.212)

(The dash in the quote above is an en dash in the book.)

> For each ¢1k you earned during this game, choose one of your surviving pilots to gain a
> Perk. Each pilot can only gain a maximum of one Perk after each game. (For example, if you
> earned ¢2k in your game, up to two of your surviving pilots can gain a Perk, even if more
> survived.) (p.212)

> When you gain a Perk, roll a D12. If you roll a Perk you already have, you can select and
> gain another Perk from your class list. (p.212)

### 3.6 Routines (p.200), verbatim

> Strongest Enemy: Pick your ship with the largest Silhouette value. If tied, pick the most
> expensive ship. If still tied, randomise.

> Nearest Enemy: Pick whichever of your ships is nearest to the active Hostile ship. If
> there's a tie, pick the tied ship with the highest cost. If still tied, randomise.

> Engage: When instructed to 'Engage' a particular target:
> Pivot this ship to face the target.
> Push this ship its Thrust value towards the target (but stop as soon as the target is in
> range of any of the active ship's weapons).
> Suffer Passive Attacks.
> Attack Smartly.

> Defend Nearest Objective: When instructed to 'Defend Nearest Objective':
> Push this ship its Thrust value towards the Objective nearest to it (but stop as soon as it
> is within 2" of that Objective).
> Pivot to face the Strongest Enemy within range of one or more of its weapons (with the
> priority of bringing its primary weapon system to bear).
> Suffer Passive Attacks.
> Attack Smartly.

> Attack Smartly: When instructed to 'Attack Smartly', for each of the active ship's primary
> and auxiliary weapon systems: assign the attack dice to its Engage target, or to the
> Strongest Enemy within range and arc if it cannot. If there are multiple equally valid
> targets, choose the ship with the most damage. If still tied, randomise.

> Clipping: If a Hostile ship or Blip token wants to occupy the same position as another
> Hostile Ship, Blip token or Objective for any reason, move it 2" towards your Entry Jump
> Point.

## 4. Interactions with the rest of the game

### 4.1 Which Era

Junkspace is its own Era. It is not a solo mode for Armageddon, the Age of Unity or
Hypergrowth, and none of those Eras' fleet lists, factions, HVPs, missions or victory
conditions are used. The book calls it "The Junkspace Era" (p.194) and situates it after the
Unhuman War, in the period the p.10 timeline labels "The Ruins". It uses its own seven ship
classes, its own currency unit (¢k, "thousands of Juran credits", p.202) and its own campaign
frame.

### 4.2 Core rules Junkspace replaces

| Core rule | Junkspace change | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Units contain up to 3 ships (p.28) | "In Junkspace, all units consist of exactly one ship." | 195 |
| Initiative Value is a faction stat (p.30) | "In other Eras, Initiative Value is a fleet-level stat. In Junkspace, it is a ship-level stat." Pilots start at 2D6. Outfit-level checks use the highest in-play value. | 197 |
| Command Phase Initiative Check decides who has Initiative, losers gain +1 CMD (p.30) | "You do not make an Initiative Check: the Hostiles activate the first ship." | 205 |
| CMD tokens per faction (p.30) | Flat 5 CMD per round, with an unspecified route to increasing it. | 205 |
| Jump Phase, players take turns, may Open a Jump Point (p.30, p.32) | Only the player acts. The two Jump Points are placed at setup. Entry is the only Jump In point; either serves for Jump Out. | 196, 205 |
| Tactical Phase, players alternate battlegroups clockwise (p.31) | Alternation is you versus the Hostiles, Hostiles first, Hostiles never form battlegroups. | 205 |
| Scan action always succeeds (p.39) | Scan requires an Initiative Check with at least one success; two or more Hostile ships within 3" of the scanning ship require one extra success. | 197 |
| End Phase (p.31) | Adds Alert Level +1 and Glitch all Blips. | 206 |
| Game end and victory by Era | Alert Level 10 or no ships in play; scoring is credits against Debt, not VP. | 201, 206, 211 |
| Command list (pp.220-221) | Adds Seize Initiative (1 CMD), Junkspace only. | 205 |

### 4.3 Core rules Junkspace keeps

Stated explicitly: "Unless noted below, assume the game works exactly as in the Core Rules."
(p.205) and "Note: Hostile Ships suffer from Inertial Strain and Easy Target as normal."
(p.200). Passive Attacks, combat resolution, Silhouette-based to-hit, shield saves, critical
hits, duds, explosion checks, Easy Target, Inertial Strain, arcs of fire and asset token
handling are all untouched.

### 4.4 Core rules Junkspace makes irrelevant

My reading, not the book's. Junkspace uses a single 3' by 3' board, so Sectors and the Jump
Hop action have no target. Squadron carriage is never mentioned, though four of the seven
player classes are Mass 0. There are no HVPs, no missions, no VP, no Bad Publicity, no
Requisition and no Shipyard. Unit Coherence and Drag To Select survive on paper but a
one-ship unit makes coherence vacuous.

## 5. Ambiguities and gaps

### 5.1 Two unfilled "page XX" cross-references

Both are the same sentence, printed twice.

> During their activations, you control the Hostiles, but you follow their Behaviour rules
> (see page XX). (p.200)

> During their activations, you control the Hostiles, but you follow their Behaviour rules
> (see page XX). (p.205)

The intended target is Hostile Behaviour on p.199. The rest of the book is riddled with
unfilled "page XX" references too (pp.20, 22, 25, 220-222 among many), so this is a
book-wide production issue rather than a Junkspace-only one.

### 5.2 The setup diagram labels both Jump Points "Entry"

The p.203 body text says:

> Place an Entry Jump Point anywhere with 3" of one corner; place the Exit Jump Point anywhere
> with 3" of the opposite corner. Divide the table diagonally in half, so that the Exit point
> is at the back of the Hostile half of the table. (p.203)

The diagram directly below it labels **both** corner markers "Entry Jump Point". I checked the
rendered page image. The top-right marker, which sits inside the shaded "HOSTILE TABLE HALF",
should by the body text be the Exit Jump Point. This matters because Jobs 9 and K pay out
specifically for jumping out through the Exit point, and the Clipping routine pushes things
"towards your Entry Jump Point".

### 5.3 Blip deployment is described twice, differently

> During setup, you shuffle the Blip markers together, deploy them facedown (without looking at
> the numbers on them) anywhere in the Hostile table half, with each at least 7" from any other
> Blip marker and 2" from all table edges. (p.197)

> Shuffle the Blip markers together, and deploy them facedown (without looking at the numbers)
> in the Hostile table half, according to this diagram. Each should be 7" from all other Blip
> markers. (p.204)

Three differences. First, "anywhere" versus "according to this diagram". Second, "at least 7""
versus "should be 7"", which reads as an exact spacing. Third, the 2" table-edge standoff
exists only on p.197. The p.204 diagram shows eight blips hugging the diagonal, which is a
very different distribution from "anywhere in the Hostile table half".

### 5.4 Alert Level 10: immediately, or at end of round?

> The game ends when the Alert Level hits 10. (p.195)

> The game ends at the end of any Round if either of the following are true: The Alert Level
> is 10 (or higher). (p.206)

These disagree whenever the Alert Level reaches 10 mid-round, which is entirely possible:
Job 7 alone adds 2 on a single scan, Job Q adds 1 per ComSat, Jobs 5 and 8 add 1, and
revealing a Mass 2-3 ship adds 1. Under p.195 the game stops on the spot. Under p.206 you
finish the round, which also lets you claw the level back down by destroying a Mass 2-3 ship
(−2) before the check. The Rule of Hard Knocks (p.195) arguably resolves this in favour of the
harsher p.195 reading, but the book does not say so.

### 5.5 Clearing your Debt both ends the campaign and sets a starting Alert Level

> If you have cleared your Debt completely, the Alert Level starts at 3. (p.195)

> If you clear your debt within 8 games, you have won the campaign. (p.201)

> When you have cleared your Debt, you win the campaign. (p.211)

If clearing the Debt wins the campaign, the Alert-Level-3 tier can never apply. Either the
campaign is meant to be playable past the win, or the tier is vestigial. The book does not
say. Note also that p.211's statement of the win condition drops the eight-game deadline that
p.201 imposes; p.201 is the only place the loss condition appears.

### 5.6 The Junker's starting perk is written for a Hauler

> Starting Perk – Smartass: A ship with a Hauler as a pilot rolls two extra dice when making an
> Initiative Check as part of a Scan action. (p.202)

Printed under the Junker heading, immediately after the Hauler's own perk which reads "A ship
with a Hauler as a pilot ignores the first damage received from each salvo." (p.202). Almost
certainly a copy-paste error; the intended word is Junker. That is my inference.

### 5.7 "Objectives" names two things that appear nowhere else

> It could be an object, such as a Facility or ComAat, or a Job-related asset token, such as a
> Kraken Heart. Carried tokens and Scrap tokens don't count as Objectives for this, they are
> invisible to the game's AI. (p.196)

"ComAat" is a typo for ComSat. But **Kraken Heart** and **Scrap tokens** appear nowhere else in
the entire 224-page book (I searched all pages). No Job produces a Kraken Heart. No rule
defines a Scrap token. The word "scraps" appears once on p.194 as flavour. So the definitional
example refers to content that was either cut or never written.

### 5.8 Job A's cap of three is unreachable with the printed Aggressor list

> For each Hostile Mass 2–3 ship you have destroyed (to a maximum of three) you earn ¢1k. If
> you have revealed and destroyed all the Hostile Mass 2–3 ships, you earn ¢3k instead. (p.207)

The Pirates table (p.211) contains exactly two Mass 2-3 ships: Blip 7, the Pirate Frigate
(Mass 2), and Blip 8, the Pirate Cruiser (Mass 3). Destroying both gives ¢3k via the second
sentence, so the "maximum of three" clause can never bind unless you use a homebrew Aggressor
list, which p.211's note invites. This is my arithmetic, not the book's.

### 5.9 Two Jobs create "Loot" tokens with no shared-pool rule

Job 6 Reclamation places a Loot token when you scan an asteroid. Job 10 Shake Down places a
Loot token when you scan a Hostile ship. Both then say "For each Loot token carried by a
friendly ship, earn ¢1k. This job can earn you a maximum of ¢3k." (pp.208-209). The book
explicitly handles double-ups for **Asteroids** ("use a single set of Asteroids shared by all
jobs", p.208 and p.209) and for the **Derelict Facility** ("use a single Derelict Facility
shared by all jobs", p.208), but says nothing about whether Loot tokens from one Job count for
the other. With both drawn, the same token plausibly pays twice.

### 5.10 Missing stat lines for several Job objects

- Job 8's Secure Facility: "The Secure Facility has Silhouette 7 and Shields 3." (p.208). No
  Mass, no Thrust, no weapons. Does it passive-attack like the Job 4 Facility does?
- Job 9's Old Unity ComSat and Job Q's ComSats: no stat line at all, no Silhouette, so they
  cannot be attacked under the core rule that "If an object has a silhouette, it is a valid
  target for attacks" (p.20).
- Asteroids: "Asteroids have Silhouette 9 and no Shields." (pp.208, 209). No Mass, which the
  core Explosion Check needs.

### 5.11 When exactly are Blips revealed?

> Blip tokens within 6" of your ships are revealed. (p.198)

The rule gives no timing. It could be a continuous check during movement, a check at the end
of the Movement Step, or a check at the end of an activation. The difference is large: a
continuous check means a ship that passes within 6" and moves out again triggers an Ambush and
ends its own activation mid-move. The Ambush rule ("your activation ends", p.198) implies the
check can fire mid-activation, but does not say when.

### 5.12 The alternating sequence is undefined when one side runs out

> The Tactical Phase works as normal, alternating the activation of battlegroups, starting with
> the Hostiles. You are free to form battlegroups as normal, but the Hostiles don't form
> battlegroups, activating one ship at a time. (p.205)

One Hostile ship trades against one whole player battlegroup, which is already asymmetric. The
book never says what happens when one side has no unactivated units left while the other still
does. The core rule is that "Once all in-play units have activated, the Tactical Phase ends."
(p.31), which suggests the side with units remaining simply continues, but this is not stated.

### 5.13 "As your outfit gains experience, this number can increase"

> Command Tokens: You gain 5 CMD tokens. As your outfit gains experience, this number can
> increase. (p.205)

No rule in the chapter increases the base 5. The only CMD-generating progression is the Junker
perk Quarterback, "+1 CMD token at the start of each round" (p.213), and the Gunner perk Kill
Confirmed (p.212). If those are what the sentence means, it is worded as though a separate
mechanic exists. Losing the Command Phase Initiative Check normally grants +1 CMD (p.30), and
Junkspace removes that check, so the player also silently loses that source.

### 5.14 Seize Initiative shares a name with a core rules heading, and is missing from the Quick Reference

The core Command Phase section on p.30 is headed "SEIZE INITIATIVE" and describes the
Initiative Check. Junkspace then introduces a command literally named "Seize Initiative (1
CMD)" (p.205) which does something different, in a mode where the Initiative Check does not
happen. The Quick Reference command list (p.221) omits it entirely, while still listing
"Requisition (1 CMD): Hypergrowth only."

### 5.15 Do Hostile Facilities count as Hostile ships?

> Hostile facilities don't attack Hostile ships. (p.195)

The Job 4/5 Derelict Facility is explicitly "Hostile" and has Laser Turrets (p.207), so it
attacks you. But several rules key off "Hostile ship" specifically: Engine Noise ("two or more
Hostile ships within 3"", p.197), Job 10 ("scan a Hostile ship", p.209), and the Alert Level
Mass 2-3 clauses. The Facility's printed Mass is 4. Whether it counts as a Hostile ship for any
of these is never stated.

### 5.16 Smaller loose ends

- **Blip 3 / Job 3 collision of terminology.** Job 3 pays out "When you reveal the last
  unrevealed Blip marker" (p.207). If a Blip is never revealed before the game ends, the Job
  simply pays nothing. That is presumably intended but is not confirmed.
- **Do destroyed pilots keep their perks?** p.212 says destroyed ships are recovered and
  repaired and that only survivors gain a Perk, but never says whether a destroyed pilot loses
  perks already earned. The optional campaign rule at p.187 does strip perks on destruction,
  and Junkspace does not reference it.
- **No mechanism to spend earnings on ships.** Every credit earned goes to the Debt (p.211).
  There is no rule for buying, replacing or upgrading ships after the initial ¢30k purchase.
- **Setup order printed twice, differently.** The numbered list on p.203 puts Blip Markers
  before Objectives; the section order on pp.203-204 puts Objectives first. The Job objects are
  Objectives, and the Blip 7" spacing is measured against other Blips only, so the practical
  impact is small, but the two orderings do not match.
- **"Track it with a D10"** (p.195) for a value that can legally reach 10 or higher (p.206).
- **Long-Range Scan says "Initiative Test"** (p.202) where every other rule in the chapter and
  the core rules say "Initiative Check". Presumably the same thing.
- **Hostiles and commands.** Nothing says Hostiles have or lack CMD tokens. The Junker perk
  Signal Jam lets you re-roll their behaviour die, and Grav Anchor affects "Hostile units", but
  no rule grants the Hostiles any command economy.
- **Squadrons.** Four of the seven player ship classes are Mass 0, which the core rules call
  Squadrons and allow to be carried (p.28). Junkspace never mentions carrying or the Scramble
  Squadrons action.

## 6. What the app would need to support Junkspace

Data structures and behaviours only, derived strictly from the rules quoted above. No UI, no
code.

### 6.1 Static data

- **Junkspace ship classes** (7 entries): name, mass, thrust, silhouette, shields, primary
  weapon system, auxiliary weapon system, cost in ¢k. Two entries have an empty primary slot
  (Starfighter, Gunship), one has an empty auxiliary slot (Bomber). The weapon model must
  allow a non-weapon in a weapon slot, because the Recon Ship's auxiliary slot holds
  "Long-Range Scanners".
- **Pilot classes** (3 entries): name, flavour text, starting perk name and rule text.
- **Perk lists** (3 lists of 12, indexed 1-12 for a D12): name, rule text, owning class. Two
  names appear on two lists each (Snapfire on Gunner and Hauler, Turbo-Spanner on Hauler and
  Junker), so perk identity is a (class, index) pair, not a name.
- **Aggressor list** (4 entries keyed by Blip number ranges 1-4, 5-6, 7, 8): ship class, mass,
  thrust, silhouette, shields, primary and auxiliary weapons, plus the faction-wide Take No
  Prisoners rule. The list must be replaceable, because p.211 explicitly invites homebrew.
- **Jobs** (13 entries keyed by playing card rank A, 2-10, J, Q, K): title, setup instructions,
  in-game triggers, end-of-game payout rule, payout cap, Alert Level modifiers, and the objects
  it requires.
- **Job objects**: Facility (Mass 4, Thrust –, Sil 7, Shields 3, Laser Turrets 3D6 0-6"),
  Secure Facility (Sil 7, Shields 3, rest unstated), Asteroid (Sil 9, Shields 0, rest
  unstated), ComSat (no stats given), Old Unity ComSat (no stats given), Hostile Gunship (from
  the Aggressor list). The model must tolerate missing stats.
- **Roll tables**: Glitch the Blip (D6, 3 outcomes), Random Behaviour (D6, 3 outcomes), Setup
  Scatter (D10 distance plus a direction from the die's pointy top).
- **Currency**: ¢k, thousands of Juran credits, distinct from the ¢bn used by every other Era.

### 6.2 Campaign state

- Debt, starting at ¢30k, or ¢45k on the harder variant.
- Games played, out of 8, or 6 on the harder variant.
- Campaign outcome: won when Debt reaches zero within the game limit, lost if Debt remains
  after the limit (and the Outfit is lost).
- Outfit roster: up to 5 ships, each with ship class, pilot class, pilot Initiative Value
  (starts at 2D6, modifiable by the Slick and Rogue perks), and an ordered list of earned
  perks. Ships are never permanently destroyed.
- Derived starting Alert Level from remaining Debt (1, 2 or 3 per the p.195 tiers).
- Purchase validation at creation: total ship cost at most ¢30k, at most 5 ships.

### 6.3 Per-game state

- The three drawn Jobs, dealt without replacement from a 13-card suit.
- Alert Level, an integer starting at the campaign-derived value, with the full modifier set in
  section 3.11, and a termination check.
- Round counter, with no fixed round limit.
- Eight Blip markers, each with a hidden number 1-8, a table position, a revealed flag, and a
  peeked flag for Long-Range Scan.
- Hostile ships in play, each with class, position, heading, damage, activated flag, and
  provenance (which Blip revealed it, or which Job spawned it).
- Player ships in play, each with position, heading, damage, activated flag, Easy Target flag,
  and carried asset tokens.
- CMD pool, 5 per round plus perk modifiers, discarded in the End Phase.
- Job progress counters: per-Job payout accrued, per-Job caps, per-Round scan limits (Job 4),
  first-time flags (Jobs 5, 7, 8, Q), token production flags per asteroid and per Hostile ship
  (Jobs 6, 10).
- Objectives register, since three separate rules ask for "the Objective nearest to it".

### 6.4 Behaviours

- **Setup**: place two Jump Points at opposite corners within 3"; divide the board on the
  diagonal; place Job objects with their spacing constraints (7" from other Objectives, 12"
  from the Exit point for Jobs 8 and 9, board centre for Jobs 4 and 5); place 8 Blips in the
  Hostile half at 7" spacing and 2" from edges; then scatter every Objective and Blip D10" with
  a random direction, stopping 2" from an edge. Jump Points are not scattered.
- **Round loop**: Command Phase (grant 5 CMD plus modifiers, no Initiative Check), Jump Phase
  (player only), Tactical Phase (Hostiles activate first unless Seize Initiative is paid),
  End Phase (Alert +1, Glitch every Blip, clear Activated tokens, discard CMD).
- **Hostile activation selection**: highest Silhouette among unactivated Hostiles, tie-break on
  proximity to the player's ships, then random.
- **Hostile behaviour resolution**: roll D6; resolve Engage Nearest Enemy, Engage Strongest
  Enemy, or Defend Nearest Objective; each ends in Attack Smartly. Requires implementations of
  Nearest Enemy (nearest, then highest cost, then random), Strongest Enemy (largest silhouette,
  then most expensive, then random), a move-until-in-range rule, a move-until-within-2"
  rule, a pivot-to-face-with-primary-priority rule, and target assignment per weapon system
  with a most-damage tie-break.
- **Clipping resolution**: on any positional collision between Hostile ships, Blips and
  Objectives, displace 2" towards the Entry Jump Point.
- **Blip reveal check**: any Blip within 6" of a player ship flips, looks up its number,
  becomes the mapped ship facing the nearest player ship, and Ambushes if it is the Tactical
  Phase. Ambush ends the player's current activation and passes play to the Hostiles. Reveals
  in the End Phase do not Ambush and take no actions until the new round. The Ghost perk lets
  the player decline a reveal.
- **Blip glitching**: on every End Phase for all Blips, and after any Open Fire for Blips
  within 6" of the target.
- **Scan resolution**: Initiative Check on the scanning pilot's Initiative Value, one success
  needed, two if two or more Hostile ships are within 3" of the scanning ship, plus the
  Smartass (+2 dice) and Knob-Twiddler (+6" range) modifiers.
- **Initiative Check for the Outfit**: highest Initiative Value among in-play ships.
- **Alert Level triggers**: wired to reveals, kills, End Phase, and the four Job triggers.
- **Game end check**: Alert Level 10 or higher, or no player ships in play. The app has to pick
  one of the two contradictory timings in section 5.4, and should say which.
- **Scoring**: evaluate each drawn Job's payout at the moment it fires (Jobs 3, 4, Q pay
  in-game; the rest pay at end of game), respect each Job's cap, sum, subtract from Debt.
- **Post-game**: award one perk per ¢1k earned, each to a different surviving pilot, at most one
  per pilot; roll D12 on the pilot's class list; on a duplicate, let the player choose any
  unowned perk from that list.
- **The Rule of Hard Knocks**: wherever the app has to resolve an ambiguity, it should resolve
  it against the player, and the mode's tone suggests surfacing that it did so.

### 6.5 Things the app cannot derive from the rules

If the app models these, it is house-ruling, and should say so:

- Which of the two Jump Points the p.203 diagram means.
- Whether Blip placement is free or diagram-constrained.
- Whether the Alert Level ends the game immediately at 10 or at end of round.
- Whether Loot tokens are shared between Jobs 6 and 10.
- Stat lines for the Secure Facility's Mass and weapons, ComSats, and Asteroid Mass.
- The exact timing of the Blip reveal check.
- What happens when one side exhausts its activations first.
- How the base 5 CMD is meant to grow.
- Whether Hostile Facilities count as Hostile ships.
